---
title: Connect a Google Calendar
description: Connect Google Calendar to a workspace, manage multiple accounts and reconnect when a sign-in expires.
---

This guide shows you how to connect Google Calendar to a workspace. It's
per-workspace and per-member: it syncs your events for that workspace into
Control Center, where you can also answer invitations, book, move and delete
events. See [Calendar and scheduling](/manual/concepts/calendar/)
for the full picture.

## Prerequisites

- The server can sign people in to Google: either through Control Center Connect,
  or with the server's own Google client
  ([Bring your own Google client](#bring-your-own-google-client)). The server
  owner chooses in **Settings → Server → Single sign-on**
- A workspace open: a calendar account always belongs to one

## Connect an account

1. Open **Calendar** in the sidebar.
2. Click **Add calendar account** in the calendar sidebar. (The same section lives at **Settings → You → Profile**, under **Google Calendar**.)
3. Click **Connect Google Calendar**. Your browser opens Google's consent page.
4. Choose the account and approve access to your calendar events and calendar list.

How it finishes depends on who runs the sign-in:

- **The server's own Google client:** Google sends your browser back to the
  server and the dialog closes on its own
- **Control Center Connect:** the browser page shows an 8-character code. Type
  it into the dialog, or let the browser open Control Center through the
  `control-center://` link and skip typing it

Your events begin syncing straight away. The server, not your device, finishes
the exchange and keeps the credential.

The account is connected **to the active workspace only**, and it is yours: two
members who connect the same Google address get separate accounts. Switch
workspaces and you'll see that workspace's calendars instead.

The connect flow requests `calendar.events` and `calendar.calendarlist.readonly`
plus `openid email`, whichever client runs it. An account connected before
Control Center could write to Google has only the read-only scope; it asks you
to reconnect, and the new sign-in grants the rest.

## Book, move, answer and delete events

- **Book an event:** in the week or day view, drag across the time grid to draw
  the span (in the month view, drag across days for an all-day event). A card
  opens beside it with the title, times, guests, a Google Meet link, location,
  description and which calendar to book on. **Save** books it on Google and
  opens it. The **+** button in the calendar header opens the same card for the
  next free quarter hour. Press Escape to drop it
- **Move an event:** drag it to another time or day, or drag its bottom edge
  to change when it ends. It opens once Google has the new time; if Google
  refuses, it goes back where it was. You can move your own events and the
  ones you organize; an invitation stays where its organizer put it
- **Answer an invitation:** open the event and choose **Yes**, **Maybe** or
  **No**
- **Delete an event:** open it and click the trash icon. The confirmation says
  who is affected: guests are told an event you organize is cancelled, while
  deleting an invitation removes it from your calendar only. Deleting one
  occurrence of a recurring event leaves the rest of the series

Guests receive Google's own email invitation, update or cancellation, and
moving one occurrence of a recurring event leaves the rest of the series. You can
only book on calendars your account may write to, and only with your own
accounts: another member's connected calendar is never offered.

## How syncing works

The **server** owns the schedule: it sweeps every workspace's connected accounts
every two minutes and writes the events into the workspace's database. Clients
never talk to Google — they read what the server wrote.

The sweep is incremental. The first pass per calendar fetches a rolling window
(60 days back, 90 days ahead) and captures Google's sync token; later passes
present that token and receive only what changed, which is what makes a
two-minute cadence affordable. The window is re-anchored with a full sync every
24 hours and whenever Google invalidates the token.

Credentials live server-side in `secrets.json` under the server's data
directory, written owner-only (`0600`) — not in the OS keychain and never on a
client device. A sign-in through Connect stores only a sealed credential that
Connect can open for this server alone; its access token stays in the server's
memory.

## Connect multiple accounts

A workspace can connect several Google accounts (for example work and personal).
Repeat the connect step for each; their events merge into the same month / week /
agenda views. Each account syncs and refreshes independently and each can be
disconnected on its own.

## Reconnect an expired account

If Google permanently invalidates an account's sign-in (a revoked or long-expired
session), syncing stops and Control Center shows a **Reconnect** banner on the
calendar, plus a desktop notification. Click **Reconnect** and sign in again;
the banner clears on the next successful sync.

The same banner appears when the sign-in can no longer be used by this server:
the server owner switched Connect servers or rotated the Connect key, the
server's own Google client changed, or the data directory was restored onto
another machine.

## Disconnect an account

From the calendar sidebar (or **Settings → You → Profile**), click
**Disconnect** on the account. This deletes its synced events and clears its
stored credential on the server.

## Bring your own Google client

Run your own Google client when you chose **Use my own apps**. The server's own
client applies only then: with Control Center's or a custom Connect, Connect
runs every lane, Google included, and the server's client stays saved but
unused. To have the consent screen name your own project while the server uses
Connect, put the workspace on its own apps and give it its own client
([below](#give-one-workspace-its-own-google-client)); that applies whatever the
server's mode.

1. In the [Google Cloud Console](https://console.cloud.google.com/apis/credentials), enable the **Google Calendar API** and configure the **OAuth consent screen**, adding the `calendar.events`, `calendar.calendarlist.readonly`, `openid` and `email` scopes. While the app is unverified, add yourself as a **test user**.
2. Create an **OAuth client ID** of application type **Web application**.
3. Add the authorized redirect URI `<server origin>/oauth/google/callback`. **Settings → Server → Single sign-on → Provider apps → Google** shows the exact URL under **Callback URLs**; copy it verbatim.
4. Paste the **Client id** and **Client secret** into that same Google block, or put them in the server's `.env` file as `GOOGLE_OAUTH_CLIENT_ID` and `GOOGLE_OAUTH_CLIENT_SECRET`.

The server refuses to start when `GOOGLE_OAUTH_CLIENT_SECRET` is exported in its
process environment; keep it in the `.env` file (in Docker, mount it at
`/app/bin/.env`). The secret never leaves the server: no RPC response carries it.
A client id without its secret is an unfinished setup, and Google sign-ins are
refused until both are set.

Changing to a different client signs everyone out of Google Calendar: a sign-in
belongs to the client that issued it.

## Give one workspace its own Google client

A workspace can connect Google Calendar through a client of its own instead of
the server's. Only a workspace admin or owner can set it up.

1. Create a Google **Web application** client as in steps 1 and 2 above
2. Go to **Settings → Workspace → Integrations**. On the **Control Center
   Connect** card, choose **Use my own apps** and click **Apply**
3. Under Calendar, the **Google Calendar app** card appears. Add the redirect
   URI it shows under **Callback URL** to the client, verbatim
4. Paste the **Client id** and **Client secret**

Calendar accounts connected in this workspace then go through that client,
whichever way the server signs people in. It never falls back to the server's
client or to Connect: until both fields are set, Google Calendar is off in this
workspace and the card says what is missing. The secret never leaves the server.

**Use my own apps** covers every provider at once: GitHub, Linear and GIF search
also run only on the workspace's own apps. Switching the workspace's sign-in
apps, or changing the workspace's client id, marks every Google account in this
workspace for reconnection. The saved fields stay when the workspace switches
back to another choice, but are used only under **Use my own apps**. Other
workspaces on the server are not affected.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom                                             | Likely cause                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Connect Google Calendar** says sign-in isn't set up | The server has neither its own Google client nor Connect. The server owner picks one in **Settings → Server → Single sign-on**    |
| Google shows `redirect_uri_mismatch`                | The redirect registered on the client does not match the **Callback URLs** value exactly, or the server's public URL changed       |
| `invalid_client` from Google                        | The id and secret are from different clients, or the client was deleted                                                            |
| Approval succeeds but sync is empty                 | Your account is not a **test user** on the consent screen, or a requested scope was not granted                                    |
| The dialog says the code does not match             | Type the 8 characters exactly as the browser shows them. Five wrong codes end the sign-in; start again                               |
| "Couldn't update your response", or booking, moving or deleting fails | The account did not grant calendar write access. Reconnect it and approve every requested scope; a calendar shared with you read-only also refuses writes |
| Everybody has to reconnect                          | The server's or the workspace's Google client changed, or the server's or the workspace's Connect choice, or the server's key, changed. See [Reconnect an expired account](#reconnect-an-expired-account) |
